What this is

A decision-support system for polar route planning, built as one pipeline.

Three coupled models take raw satellite and ocean observations and turn them into a single, actionable route recommendation — forecast the ice, predict where the hazards drift, then choose the path that balances safety against fuel and time.

Built for Smart India Hackathon 2026. This is a working research prototype, not a certified navigation system. Every capability below is labelled as implemented, simulated, or planned — nothing here claims real-world collision avoidance.

How it works

Observe, forecast, assess, plan.

Four stages, each feeding the next.

01

Observe

Raw polar data, ingested

Passive-microwave imagery, synthetic-aperture radar, ocean reanalysis, and weather feeds are pulled in and normalized into a common spatial grid.

02

Forecast

Sea-ice concentration, ahead of time

A spatio-temporal ConvLSTM model projects sea-ice concentration forward across the grid, with a confidence value attached to each forecast.

03

Assess risk

Iceberg drift, and where it gets dangerous

A physics model corrected with ML and Kalman filtering produces a drift cone per iceberg. Combined with sea-ice forecasts and proximity, this becomes a dynamic risk map.

04

Plan

The route that balances risk and cost

A* / Dijkstra search runs over the risk-weighted grid to recommend the safest route that doesn't burn unnecessary fuel or time on avoidable detours.
